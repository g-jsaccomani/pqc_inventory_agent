## 1. Questionnaire Overview, Consultative Rigor & Architectural Integration

An automated cloud control-plane scan reveals what cryptographic algorithms are deployed (e.g., RSA-2048, ECDSA P-256, AES-256-GCM), but cannot by itself know the statutory confidentiality lifespan of the underlying business data, the contractual constraints of third-party SaaS peers, or the firmware replacement cycle of on-premises hardware. This questionnaire bridges that gap with rigorous, evidence-backed posture verification depth.

### 1.1 Consultative Readiness Assessment vs. Statutory Certification Boundary

Important Engagement Framing: Google Cloud Consulting (PSO) conducts a Consultative PQC Readiness Assessment, Cryptographic Inventory (CBOM), and Posture Verification—never a formal statutory or regulatory certification. However, to ensure the customer's engineering and executive teams are fully prepared for external regulatory scrutiny, every domain in this questionnaire is structured with verification-grade rigor, concrete evidence artifacts, and direct control traceability aligned with NIST FIPS 203/204/205, NIST IR 8547 (Final, Oct 2026), White House EO 14412, OMB M-26-15, NSA CNSA 2.0, UK NCSC (2028/2031/2035), EU DORA, and PCI-DSS v4.0.

### 1.2 How This Questionnaire Connects to the Technical & Executive Suite

1. Feeds the Calibration Ledger (Agentic PQC Technical Architecture and Consultant Guide): Consultant findings from Domains 1-7 are entered via structured Calibration Prompts; the agent records the sector profile, Mosca parameters and gateway header limits with calibrate-assessment-context(), persisting in the transactional PostgreSQL calibration ledger (`pqc_calibration_events`) with each value marked as stakeholder-provided or sector baseline.
2. Calibrates the Quantum Risk Analysis (Agentic PQC Technical Architecture and Consultant Guide): Domain 2 defines X (Data Confidentiality Shelf-Life in years); Domains 4-7 define Y (Engineering & Infrastructure Refactoring Duration in years); Domain 1 calibrates Z (CRQC Risk Appetite Horizon). These answers are recorded as Mosca parameters (X data shelf-life, Y migration time, Z threat horizon) and drive Mosca's Inequality (X + Y > Z), computed with auditable arithmetic. Domain 2 breach-cost and data-volume answers (Q2.4) are used (optionally) to size business impact with your own data, presented as ranges with visible assumptions; the agent never invents dollar figures.
3. Governs Target Reference Architecture Selection (Agentic PQC Technical Architecture and Consultant Guide & Agentic PQC Security Recommendations Book): Answers regarding FIPS 140-3 Level 3 mandates, sovereign key control (BYOK/EKM), and VPC Service Controls directly determine the recommended 5-product Google Cloud Security BOM.

### 3.4 Domain 4 — Application Runtime, Cryptographic SDKs & API Breaking-Change Readiness (Weight: 20%)

4. Q4.4 — Database & Persistent Storage Column Byte-Width Constraints: Do relational or NoSQL databases (Cloud SQL, Spanner, AlloyDB, Bigtable) store ciphertexts, public keys, or digital signatures in fixed-width columns (e.g., VARCHAR(256), VARBINARY(512)) that will truncate or throw SQL overflow errors when storing 1,120-byte KEM-XWING ciphertexts or 3,309-byte ML-DSA-65 signatures? [Evidence: DDL Schema Review]
